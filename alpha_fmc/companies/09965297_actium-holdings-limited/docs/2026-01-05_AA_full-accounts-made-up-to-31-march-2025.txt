STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 MARCH 2025

introduction

The Directors present the Company's Strategic Report for the year ended 31 March 2025.

Business review

The Company, Actium Holdings Limited, was formerly named Alpha Financial Markets Consulting plc and was
listed on AIM, a market operated by the London Stock Exchange. During the year, the Company was acquired
by Actium Bidco (UK) Limited, an indirect subsidiary of certain funds managed by Bridgepoint Advisers Limited.
Upon completion of the acquisition, the Company's shares were delisted from AIM, the Company was re-
registered as a private limited company, and its name was changed to Actium Holdings Limited. This has been
a pivotal period for the business, with Bridgepoint's investment representing a strong endorsement of the
Group's strength and future potential. 7

Following the acquisition, the Company's new ultimate parent undertaking is Actium Topco (UK) Limited. Actium
Topco (UK) Limited prepares consolidated financial statements which include the Company. Accordingly, the
Company is no longer required to prepare consolidated financial statements itself, nor to comply with the
regulatory and governance framework applicable to public companies. These financial statements are therefore
prepared on the basis of private company individual accounts and do not consolidate the results of the
Company's subsidiaries.

During the year, all outstanding share options in the Company were exercised prior to the Bridgepoint
acquisition. As a result, the remaining share-based payment charge in respect of these options was recognised
in full, amounting to £8.4m. The majority of this charge was recharged to the Company's subsidiaries, with only
a small portion recognised in the Company’s statement of comprehensive income in respect of its Directors.

The Company recognised an interest expense of £2.2m (2024: £0.9m) relating to its former revolving credit
facility (now repaid and closed) and to the newly issued preference shares, which are accounted for as debt.
This was partly offset by £1.2m (2024: £NIL) of interest income on intercompany loans. After tax, the Company
recorded a loss for the year of £0.9m (2024: profit of £21.0m).

The Company maintains a strong financial position, with net assets of £168.1m (2024: £160.4m). Its most
significant assets comprise investments in subsidiaries and amounts due from other group undertakings, while
the principal liabilities relate to amounts owed to other group undertakings.

The Company now serves as an intermediate holding company within the group headed by Actium Topco (UK)
Limited and is expected to continue in this role for the foreseeable future.

Key performance indicators

The Directors review the management accounts on a regular basis and compare performance against budget.
Utilisation, rates and costs are also monitored to assess profitability.

The Company monitors cash flow, liquidity, and the recovery of trade receivables primarily through regular
reviews of working capital across its subsidiaries. The Company itself maintains a strong balance sheet, with
net assets of £168.1m (2024: £160.4m).

Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risks and uncertainties faced by the Company and its subsidiaries are outlined below:

Competitors — Risk that a competitor or new entrant may be able to achieve success and win work from
the Company's existing clients. Less mature or more specialist propositions, which the Company chooses to
develop, may present less of a competitive advantage.

Macro — Risk that wider market, economic, commercial and financial risks can impact the Company's ability
to service clients, their demand for consultancy and technology services and, therefore, the Company’s own
performance and financial position.

Quality of service — Failure to maintain quality of service on client delivery engagements.

People — Risk of failure to attract, incentivise and retain the best people with the right capabilities across all
propositions, levels and geographies. A failure to undertake effective succession planning for key roles
could also adversely increase this risk and impact performance.

Business strategy — Risk that the Company responds inadequately to changing market factors, including
technological advancements, and strategic growth opportunities, including acquisitions, The success of the
Group depends on continuing to develop a proposition that best serves its clients.

Security — Risk of cyber-attacks, security breaches and data mishandling, leading to consequent risks to
client data, loss of integrity or availability of core data.

Going concern

In assessing the Company's ability to continue on a going concer basis for a period of at least 12
months from the approval of these financial statements (the “going concer period’), the Directors
considered the Company's projected cash flows, access to intercompany funding from other group
entities, cash liquidity and existing borrowing facilities.

During the year, following the de-listing of the Company from AIM,.the Company transitioned to be
an intermediate holding company within the group, with no further trading expected through the
going concern period. The Company is in a net assets position, primarily comprising amounts
receivable from other group entities. As such, the Company is well positioned to meet its financial
liabilities as they fall due, albeit there is a reliance upon the ability of other group companies to settle

recoverability of these intercompany balances. Further, the Company has access to further support
through other group entities, and the group's revolving credit facility.

The Directors have therefore considered the cash flow forecasts prepared for the wider group, which
support the group's ability to support the Company through the going concer period, if required.
These group cash flow forecasts cover the going concem period and are based upon the Board-
approved budget for the group and have been appropriately sensitised, as further disclosed in the
consolidated Actium Topco (UK) Limited financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2025.
These forecasts demonstrate the group has sufficient resources to settle liabilities as they fall due.

After careful consideration, the Directors have a reasonable expectation that the Company's existing
resources, supplemented by the support of the wider group, are adequate to meet its requirements
over the going concer period. On this basis, the Directors have adopted the going concem basis in
preparing the financial statements

2.9, Share-based payments

The cost of share-based employee compensation arrangements, whereby employees receive
remuneration in the form of shares or share options, is recognised as an employee benefit expense
in the profit & loss account. ,
The total expense to be apportioned over the vesting period of the benefit is determined by
reference to the fair value (excluding the effect of non-market-based vesting conditions) at the date
of grant.

At the end of each reporting period the assumptions underlying the number of awards expected to
vest are adjusted for the effects of non-market-based vesting conditions to reflect the conditions
prevailing at that date. The impact of any revisions to the original estimates is recognised in profit or
loss, with a corresponding adjustment to equity. Fair value is measured by the use of a binomial
model. The assumptions have been adjusted, based on management's best estimate, for the effects
of non- transferability, lack of dividend until vesting and exercise restrictions.

After vesting, the Company satisfies share option exercises either through the issuance of new
ordinary shares, or through the transfer of existing shares held in the Company's employee benefit
trust to the employee.

An associated employers’ social security tax liability on these share options is accrued through the
vesting period to align with the payment due upon exercise.

The Company applies an intra-group recharge arrangement to the share-based payments charge
relating to employees of subsidiary companies, to reflect the benefits received by the respective
entity in relation to employees granted share options.

The Company is deemed to be the settling entity in the intra-group arrangement, as share options
granted are in relation to ordinary shares of the Company, and recognises the share-based
payments charge for the full group in other reserves. The Company's subsidiaries are considered to
be the receiving entities in the arrangement, in line with the benefit received for services provided
through ongoing employment.

Amounts relating to employees who provide services directly to the Company are recorded as an
equity-settled share-based payment charge through the Company's statement of comprehensive
income and are not recharged.

The remaining charge in relation to employees who provide services to subsidiary companies is
initially recognised as an investment in subsidiaries and is simultaneously reversed upon the
recharge of this cost to the receiving entity through amounts owed by Group undertakings. This
charge is recognised within the profit and loss of the relevant receiving entities.

Deferred tax balances are recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not

. The recognition of deferred tax assets is limited to the extent that it is probable that they will

Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of

Financial liabilities
Fair value through profit or loss

Financial liabilities are classified as at fair value through profit or loss, when the financial liability is
held for trading, or is designated as at fair value through profit or loss. This designation may be
made if such designation eliminates or significantly reduces a measurement or recognition
inconsistency that would otherwise arise, or the financial liability forms part of a group of financial
instruments which is managed and its performance is evaluated on a fair value basis, or the financial
liability forms part of a contract containing one or more embedded derivatives, and IFRS 9 permits
the entire combined contract to be designated as at fair value through profit or loss. Any gains or
losses arising on changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss to the extent that they are not
part of a designated hedging relationship.

At amortised cost

Financial fiabilities which are neither contingent consideration of an acquirer in a business
combination, held for trading, nor designated as at fair value through profit or loss are subsequently
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method. This is a method of calculating the
amortised cost of a financial liability and of allocating interest expense over the relevant period. The
effective interest rate is the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash payments through the
expected life of the financial liability, or where appropriate a shorter period, to the amortised cost of a
financial liability.
